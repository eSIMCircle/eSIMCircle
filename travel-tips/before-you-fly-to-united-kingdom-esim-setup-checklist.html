United Kingdom eSIM checklist: preparing your phone before the trip

UK cities run on contactless payments and phone maps. Arriving at Heathrow or Gatwick with data already working means you can head for the train instead of the Wi-Fi login page.

Published September 21, 2026 · Updated October 9, 2026 · 3 min read

Phone checks

Start a week ahead.

  • eSIM support: look for an EID in your phone's About screen (iPhone: Settings > General > About).
  • Carrier lock: on iPhone, the Carrier Lock line should say "No SIM restrictions". On Android, check with your carrier.
  • Software: install pending updates at home rather than in your first hotel.

If anything fails here, contact your carrier now. A carrier unlock cannot be done from the airport.

Choosing the plan

Count the days you need data, including arrival and departure. A London-only trip with lots of walking and Tube journeys uses steady data for maps and timetables. A trip that adds Edinburgh, York or the countryside by train adds long journeys where you may want to stream or work.

NotePlanning to work from a laptop on hotspot? Check the hotspot rules on the plan page before you rely on them.

Installing

  1. Connect to home Wi-Fi.
  2. Scan the QR code from your eSIMCircle email.
  3. Name the line "UK".
  4. Leave it off for data until you land.
  5. Save the email or a screenshot of the QR code where you can open it offline.

Getting around

In London you can tap a contactless bank card or phone wallet on the Tube, buses and many trains, so you do not need to buy a separate travel card on day one. If you use a phone wallet, make sure it is set up and working before you leave home.

The night before

  • Download offline maps for the cities on your route.
  • Screenshot train tickets and hotel confirmations.
  • Turn data roaming off on your home SIM.

After landing

Set the UK eSIM as your mobile data line and switch on data roaming for it. Check a map. If the eSIM shows no service after a few minutes, toggle airplane mode or restart the phone. Leave the profile installed while you troubleshoot.

Troubleshooting questions

Can I use contactless payment on the London Underground?

Yes. Contactless bank cards and phone wallets work on the Tube and buses, which is easier than buying a ticket on arrival.

How much data do I need for London?

Maps, transit times and messaging use steady but modest data. Heavy users who stream or tether a laptop should choose a larger or daily plan.

What if my phone is carrier-locked?

A locked phone will not accept a travel eSIM. Ask your carrier to unlock it before you travel.
