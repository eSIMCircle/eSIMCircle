How to install an eSIM for Thailand when it is one of several on your phone

Thailand is often one stop on a longer Asia trip. If your phone ends up holding two or three travel eSIMs, clear labels and a simple switching routine stop you paying for the wrong one.

Published April 19, 2026 · Updated September 18, 2026 · 3 min read

First, decide: one plan or several?

If your route covers Thailand plus neighbours such as Malaysia or Singapore, a multi-country plan may cover all of them on one eSIM. Check the coverage list on the plan page. If your route mixes regions, you may end up with a separate eSIM for each.

Installing the Thailand eSIM

On home Wi-Fi:

  • iPhone: Settings > Mobile Data > Add eSIM, then scan the QR code from the email.
  • Android: Settings > Network & internet or Connections > SIMs > Add eSIM, then scan.

Label everything

Phones list eSIMs by label, and default labels such as "Secondary" or "Travel" are easy to mix up. Name each one after the country:

LineLabelUsed for
Home SIMHomeCalls and SMS throughout
Thailand eSIMThailandData while in Thailand
Next country's eSIMe.g. VietnamData in that country

Switching between countries

When you cross into a new country:

  1. Turn data roaming off on the eSIM you are leaving.
  2. Select the new country's eSIM for mobile data.
  3. Turn roaming on for that eSIM.
  4. Open a map to test.
NoteA travel eSIM will not connect in a country outside its coverage list. Switching lines cleanly at each border avoids confusion about which one is working.

Tidying up afterwards

When a plan has expired and you are sure you will not top it up, you can remove it from the phone. Phones can store several eSIMs, but a shorter list makes the next trip easier. Keep any eSIM you might extend.

Arrival in Thailand

Set the Thailand line for data and turn on roaming for it. If nothing loads after a few minutes, restart and check the data line. Contact support before deleting anything.

Troubleshooting questions

Can my phone hold more than one travel eSIM?

Yes. Most eSIM phones can store several profiles, though only one or two can be active at a time depending on the model.

Should I delete my old travel eSIMs?

Only when they have expired and you are sure you will not top them up or extend them.

Is one plan enough for Thailand and Malaysia?

It can be, if you choose a plan whose coverage list includes both.
