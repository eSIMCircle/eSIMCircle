Germany eSIM setup checklist for the days before your flight

Germany rewards travelers who arrive with data already working, especially if the trip involves trains between cities. This checklist runs from one week out to the moment you land.

Published August 3, 2025 · Updated September 8, 2026 · 3 min read

Seven days out: the phone

Start with the two checks that can hold everything up.

  • Check that the phone supports eSIM. Most recent iPhone, Pixel and Galaxy models do, but some regional versions do not. On iPhone, an EID under Settings > General > About means the phone has an eSIM chip.
  • Check that it is not carrier-locked. A phone bought on contract may still be locked. If the Carrier Lock line does not say "No SIM restrictions", contact your carrier now, not the night before.

Five days out: the plan

Work out the number of days you need data. Count the arrival day and the departure day in full.

Think about how you will move around. If you are taking long-distance trains between Berlin, Munich, Hamburg or Cologne, you will likely check connections, platform changes and tickets on your phone through the day. Signal can also drop between stations in rural stretches, so a few saved offline maps help.

Two days out: install

  1. Connect to home Wi-Fi.
  2. Scan the QR code from the eSIMCircle email (iPhone: Settings > Mobile Data > Add eSIM; Android: Settings > Network or Connections > SIM manager).
  3. Label the line "Germany".
  4. Leave it off for mobile data for now.

Save the QR email offline. If you ever need support, they will ask for details from it.

Departure day

  • Turn data roaming off for your home SIM.
  • Download offline maps for your first city and your train route.
  • Keep the eSIMCircle email handy.

Arrival

At Frankfurt, Munich, Berlin or wherever you land, set the eSIM as your mobile data line and turn on data roaming for that line. Give it a couple of minutes to find a network. If it does not, restart the phone before changing anything else.

Plans and prices change, so check the current Germany options before you install.

Troubleshooting questions

Does an eSIM work on German trains?

It works wherever the partner network has coverage. Expect occasional gaps between stations in rural areas and download offline maps for long rail days.

Should I buy a daily plan or a fixed plan for Germany?

Fixed plans suit trips with predictable use. Daily plans suit trips where you use a lot of data every day, such as remote work or heavy navigation.

What if I run out of data halfway through the trip?

Top up or extend the plan from your phone. Every eSIMCircle plan supports top-up and extension while it is active.
