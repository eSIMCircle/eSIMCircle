Before You Fly to Germany: eSIM Setup Checklist

This pre-flight checklist is for travelers heading to Dresden, Germany. It covers compatibility, installation, activation, APN checks and the small settings that are easiest to fix before departure.

Before you start

Do the setup while you still have dependable Wi-Fi and access to your order email. For a trip to Dresden, Germany, that means checking the phone before travel day rather than discovering a compatibility or lock issue after arrival.

  • Confirm eSIM support. The phone needs an eSIM-capable model and software version.
  • Confirm the phone is unlocked. A carrier-locked device may reject another provider’s eSIM.
  • Keep the QR code or manual activation details. Save the email somewhere you can reopen on another screen if needed.
  • Know which SIM is your home line. Label the travel eSIM clearly so you do not enable roaming on the wrong line.
  • Install on stable Wi-Fi. Do not leave the first installation attempt until you are standing at an airport curb or station platform.

Device compatibility checks

Menu names can vary by phone brand and software version. On iPhone, look under Settings → Cellular/Mobile Data. On many Android phones, look under Settings → Network & Internet or Connections → SIMs/SIM Manager. The goal is the same: confirm an eSIM can be added and that the device is not carrier-locked.

1
Can the phone add an eSIM?Look for “Add eSIM”, “Add Cellular Plan”, or an equivalent SIM-manager option.
2
Is it carrier-unlocked?eSIM capability and carrier-unlock status are separate checks.
3
Is the software current?Install pending system updates before adding the travel profile when practical.
4
Can you keep two lines?If you want your home number available for SMS, check how your phone handles dual-SIM use.

Important: do not delete an installed travel eSIM just because data is not working yet. First check line selection, roaming and APN settings. A deleted profile may not be reusable.

Installation steps for iPhone and Android

iPhone

  1. Connect to Wi-Fi and open Settings → Cellular/Mobile Data → Add eSIM.
  2. Scan the QR code from the order email, or choose the manual-entry option if you were given activation details.
  3. Label the new line something clear such as Germany Travel.
  4. Keep your normal SIM as the default voice/SMS line if you still need your regular number.
  5. When you are ready to use the travel plan, select the eSIM for mobile data and follow the provider’s roaming instructions.

Android

  1. Connect to Wi-Fi and open Settings → Network & Internet or Connections → SIM Manager.
  2. Choose the option to add/download an eSIM, then scan the QR code or enter the activation information manually.
  3. Give the line a recognizable travel label.
  4. Choose the eSIM as the mobile-data line when you are ready to use it.
  5. If the plan instructions require roaming, enable roaming for the travel eSIM only.

Exact wording differs between Samsung, Pixel and other Android phones, so follow the intent rather than relying on one menu name.

Activation and troubleshooting

Installation adds the profile; activation is the point at which the travel line is actually used. For Dresden, keep the distinction clear so you do not accidentally send data through your home SIM.

Before arrival

Install the eSIM, label it, save the QR/manual details, and keep your home line’s data roaming off. You can leave the travel line disabled until you are ready if your plan instructions call for activation at destination.

When ready to connect

Turn on the travel eSIM, select it for mobile data, enable roaming for that line if instructed, then open a simple webpage or mapping app to test data.

If the phone offers “Allow Cellular Data Switching” or a similar feature, consider turning it off so the device does not fall back to your home SIM for data without you noticing.

No signal

Check that the travel eSIM line is enabled, then toggle airplane mode or restart the phone. Automatic network selection is usually the safest first setting unless your instructions specify a network.

Signal but no data

Confirm the eSIM is the active mobile-data line, check data roaming for that line, then verify the APN against the provider’s exact instructions.

Home SIM starts using data

Turn off mobile-data switching/fallback and keep data roaming disabled on the home SIM.

QR code will not scan

Open the QR on another device or use manual activation details if provided. Do not repeatedly delete and re-add the profile.

Once data works, test the tools you will actually depend on in Germany: a browser, maps/navigation, messaging, translation, or a ride app. A successful speed test is less useful than confirming the apps you need can load.

When to contact support

Contact support after you have confirmed the basic line settings but still cannot connect, or if the eSIM will not install at all. Prepare useful details before you write so troubleshooting does not start from zero.

  • Your order number or ICCID, if available.
  • Phone make and exact model.
  • A screenshot of the SIM/eSIM settings page.
  • A screenshot of the APN page if the problem is data-related.
  • Whether the eSIM shows a network name or signal bars.
  • What you already tried, such as restart, roaming check or APN verification.

Frequently asked questions

Should I install my Germany eSIM before departure?

Usually yes. Installing on reliable Wi-Fi gives you time to solve compatibility or setup problems before travel. Follow the plan’s activation instructions so you know when actual service or validity begins.

Do I need to remove my physical SIM?

Usually no. Many eSIM-capable phones can keep the home SIM installed while the travel eSIM handles mobile data. Make sure the correct line is selected for data and that home-SIM data roaming is off.

Should I change the APN immediately?

No. First check line selection, roaming and basic connectivity. Only edit the APN if the provider’s instructions give you a specific value or support asks you to change it.

What should I save before I travel?

Keep the order email, QR code or manual activation details, order number, and any support contact information somewhere you can access without relying on the travel eSIM itself.
