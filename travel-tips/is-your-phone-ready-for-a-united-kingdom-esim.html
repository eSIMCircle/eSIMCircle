Is your phone ready for a United Kingdom eSIM?

Plenty of travelers take an older or second-hand phone abroad. Those phones raise a few extra questions: was it unlocked, does it still hold someone else's eSIM, and does the model support eSIM at all?

Published June 22, 2025 · Updated August 10, 2026 · 3 min read

A checklist for older and second-hand phones

CheckHowWhy it matters
eSIM supportiPhone: EID under Settings > General > About. Android: add-eSIM option in SIM settings, or *#06#No eSIM chip, no travel eSIM

If the phone was bought second-hand

The carrier lock belongs to the carrier that originally sold the phone. If a second-hand phone is locked, only that carrier can unlock it, and some will ask for proof of purchase. Check this early.

Leftover eSIM profiles from a previous owner are worth removing only if you are sure they are not yours and not needed. If in doubt, leave them and just label your own lines clearly.

Installing the UK eSIM

On home Wi-Fi, add the eSIM from your eSIMCircle email (iPhone: Settings > Mobile Data > Add eSIM; Android: SIM settings > Add eSIM). Label it "UK". Keep your home line for calls and texts.

NoteAn older battery and a long day of London navigation do not mix well. Carry a power bank and download offline maps.

After landing

Select the UK eSIM for mobile data and turn on roaming for it. Keep roaming off on the home SIM. If it does not connect, restart and recheck the data line; do not delete the profile.

Troubleshooting questions

Can I use a second-hand phone with a UK travel eSIM?

Yes, if it supports eSIM and is unlocked. Only the carrier that originally sold the phone can unlock it.

Should I delete old eSIMs I find on a used phone?

Only if you are sure they are not needed. Otherwise label your own lines clearly and leave the rest.

My older phone's battery drains fast. Any tips?

Carry a power bank, download offline maps and keep background app updates on Wi-Fi.
