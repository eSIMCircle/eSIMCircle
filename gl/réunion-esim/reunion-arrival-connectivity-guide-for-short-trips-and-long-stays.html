Getting connected on arrival in Réunion, for short trips and long stays

The first hour after landing in Réunion is when you most need data and least want to troubleshoot. This page covers setup for both short trips and longer stays.

Start with arrival-day connectivity

The right Réunion eSIM choice depends on the travel moment behind this page: arrival-day connectivity. Someone who only needs maps and messages can buy differently from someone using hotspot data, uploading media, or moving through several stops in one day. Treat the plan as part of the itinerary, then compare data allowance, validity, and setup timing against the real trip.

Arrival day also tends to be the heaviest data day of the trip. Navigation, ride apps and hotel messages all arrive together, before you have found Wi-Fi, so size the plan for that day first.

An eSIM is not a substitute for preparation. Save the address of your first stop, your tickets and an offline map before you leave, so a gap in signal costs minutes and not the afternoon.

Match data to the busiest travel day

For Réunion, the safest plan is usually the one that handles the most demanding day, not the average day. Count what will happen away from Wi-Fi: route searches, booking emails, ride apps, translation, payment confirmations, and quick browser checks. If arrival-day connectivity involves hotspot use or long navigation sessions, leave extra data room instead of buying the smallest option.

The first hour after landing is when a missing connection costs the most: a map, a message to whoever is meeting you, a ride, and the address of your first stop. If the eSIM is installed beforehand, all four are available the moment you switch it on.

Keep large app updates, cloud backups and long uploads for Wi-Fi. They are the quiet drain on any allowance, because phones start them the moment they see a connection.

  • Keep the QR code email until the trip ends.
  • Use Wi-Fi for updates and heavy uploads.
  • Set the eSIM as the data line at destination.

Install before the connection matters

Install the eSIM profile while you still have stable Wi-Fi. Keep the QR code email, confirm the phone is unlocked and eSIM-compatible, and decide which line will handle mobile data. At destination, select the travel eSIM for data and enable roaming for that eSIM line. If the network does not register right away, restart the phone before changing advanced settings.

Two things make installation go smoothly: a stable Wi-Fi connection and the QR email saved somewhere you can open without mobile data. Everything else, including switching the eSIM on, can wait until you land.

Use validity as part of the budget

A cheap plan can be poor value if it expires before the final transfer or runs out during the one day you need it most. Match validity to arrival and departure days, then add a small buffer for delays. For arrival-day connectivity, compare the plan cards below by total data, active period, top-up path, and whether the plan suits the whole Réunion route.

Plans are sold in steps of days, and trips rarely match them exactly. When a trip falls between two steps, the longer plan is usually the safer choice, because a plan can be extended while it is active but cannot be stretched after it ends.

A plan that ends a day early, or one that empties on the busiest day, often costs more in the end than one sized correctly from the start, because you buy a second plan to cover the gap.

Keep a simple backup routine

Before leaving the hotel or airport, save the address, first route, and any tickets you may need offline. Use Wi-Fi for app updates, cloud photo backup, and long uploads. Mobile data should stay available for the jobs that matter during travel: maps, messages, check-in details, and support access if arrival-day connectivity becomes harder than expected.

Over weeks, habit matters more than any one day. Moving large downloads and backups to Wi-Fi saves more data than any change of plan.

Questions and answers

What should I switch on after landing?

Set the eSIM as your mobile data line and turn on data roaming for that line only. Leave roaming off on your home SIM if you want to avoid charges.

What is the lowest listed price for Réunion?

The lowest price listed on this page is 1.69 USD for Réunion eSIM 500MB/Day.

What if data does not start straight away?

Give the phone a few minutes to register, then restart it. Check that the eSIM is selected for data before changing anything else.

How many plans are listed for Réunion?

14 plans: 6 daily-data and 8 fixed-data Standard plans.

Can I keep my main SIM active?

Yes. Many travelers keep the home SIM for calls and SMS, such as bank codes, and use the eSIM for data.

Where are the current price and hotspot terms?

On the plan page, which always carries the current price, coverage and hotspot terms.

Can a plan be extended mid-trip?

Yes. Every plan can be topped up with more data or extended with more days while it is still active.
